Overview

Indigo Snakes are members of the family Colubridae, which also includes many other non-venomous snakes.

They are known for their distinctive indigo coloration and docile nature.

Physical description

Indigo Snakes have elongated, slender bodies with smooth scales. Their coloration is typically a rich, dark indigo or blue-black, which can vary in intensity depending on the individual and lighting conditions.

The underside of the snake is usually lighter in color, often with a slight yellow or cream tint. Young snakes may have more pronounced coloration than adults.

Diet and feeding

Indigo Snakes are carnivorous and primarily feed on small mammals, birds, and other reptiles. They are known to be opportunistic feeders and will consume a variety of prey depending on availability.

Behavior

Indigo Snakes are generally docile and non-aggressive. They are primarily terrestrial but are also capable of climbing trees and swimming.

Habitat and range

Indigo Snakes are found in the southeastern United States, particularly in areas with dense vegetation and access to water sources. This could include Florida, Georgia, and parts of Alabama and Mississippi. They prefer habitats such as pine flatwoods, hardwood forests, and wetlands.
